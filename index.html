01 / The idea

More than a
carbon calculator.

A shared language for the tools people already use.

The 2019 CarbonKit paper imagined a common API and dashboard connecting carbon tracking with health, savings, social participation and incentives. The aim is to make information easier to understand and act on, while keeping people in control of their data.

The next step is a small, testable foundation: interoperable records, transparent calculations and private personal views. Health, savings, social and reward features would be optional extensions.

01

Connect, with permission.

Provider-held raw data stays with its provider. Connections and disclosures need clear, specific user consent.

02

Show the method.

Every estimate should carry its sources, units, factor version, boundaries and uncertainty. Missing data must stay visible.

03

Keep the goal personal.

Start with voluntary understanding and goals people choose. Reward points are distinct from carbon credits and offsets.

A loop for learning

Small steps. Useful feedback.

The paper’s proposed feedback loop connects a person’s motivation with information and the next choice. Its effects remain to be evaluated.

  1. 01

    Motivate

    Choose a reason and a goal.

  2. 02

    Track

    Connect relevant activities.

  3. 03

    Inform

    Understand estimates and gaps.

  4. 04

    Adjust

    Consider a practical change.

  5. 05

    Reward

    Recognise progress and reflect.

03 / Research roots

An idea grounded
in a shared vision.

CarbonKit: Designing A Personal Carbon Tracking Platform

Laura Guzman · Stephen Makonin · Alex Clapp

Presented at the Fourth International Workshop on Social Sensing in Montreal in 2019, the paper sets out the CarbonKit concept, five connected modules and a privacy-conscious approach to platform design.

It is a proposal, rather than evidence of a deployed service or demonstrated changes in behaviour or health. Its British Columbia examples describe a historical design case, not current project partners.

Read the original paper ↗

Related research

A Consumer Bill of Rights for Energy Conservation (2014) proposes seven rights: access, privacy, security, equality, comfort, re-entry and maximization. It informs how CarbonKit could make energy information useful and participation fair, while respecting consumer control and basic comfort.

Stephen Makonin · Laura Guzman Flores · Robyn Gill · Roger Alex Clapp · Lyn Bartram · Bob Gill

Read the consumer-rights paper ↗

Calculating the Carbon Footprint of Streaming Media: Beyond the Myth of Efficiency (2022) supplies the research behind the StreamingCalc integration candidate.

These research proposals inform the design; CarbonKit has not demonstrated the proposed outcomes.
